Condition

Chronic Pelvic Pain

Pelvic pain lasting six months or more often has more than one cause. A thorough assessment looks beyond the gynaecological organs alone.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

Chronic pelvic pain is pain in the lower abdomen or pelvis that has lasted for six months or longer. It may be constant or come and go, and may or may not follow the menstrual cycle. Several contributing factors are often present at once.

Common symptoms

  • Aching, cramping or sharp pain in the lower abdomen or pelvis
  • Pain that worsens with periods, intercourse, or bladder or bowel function
  • Pain when sitting or after activity
  • Effects on sleep, mood and daily life

Possible causes

Gynaecological

  • Endometriosis and adenomyosis
  • Pelvic adhesions or previous pelvic infection
  • Ovarian cysts and fibroids, in some cases

Non-gynaecological

  • Pelvic floor muscle dysfunction
  • Bladder pain syndrome
  • Irritable bowel syndrome and other bowel conditions
  • Musculoskeletal and nerve-related pain

In some patients no single structural cause is found; the pain is still real and can still be treated.

Diagnostic approach

A careful history is the most important step, followed by examination — including assessment of the pelvic floor muscles — and a detailed pelvic ultrasound. Further tests are arranged only when they are likely to change management. Laparoscopy is considered selectively.

Treatment choices

  • Treating an identified cause, such as endometriosis
  • Medication, including hormonal treatment and medicines for nerve-related pain
  • Pelvic floor physiotherapy
  • Input from pain medicine, gastroenterology, urology or psychology when helpful

Surgery is offered only when there is a clear indication. Improvement is often gradual and usually comes from combining approaches.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • Pain that has lasted more than a few months
  • Pain affecting work, relationships or sleep
  • Pain continuing after previous treatment or surgery

Frequently asked questions

Does chronic pelvic pain always mean endometriosis?

No. Endometriosis is one common cause, but muscle, bladder, bowel and nerve-related causes are also frequent, and several can coexist.

Will I need a laparoscopy?

Not always. Laparoscopy is considered when the history, examination and imaging suggest it is likely to help.

What if my tests are normal?

Normal tests are common and do not mean nothing can be done. Treatment then focuses on the pain mechanisms themselves.
