Condition

Urogynecology

Urogynaecology is the care of bladder and pelvic floor problems in women — most commonly urinary leakage and pelvic organ prolapse.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles and ligaments that supports the bladder, uterus, vagina and bowel. When it is weakened or injured, problems with bladder control, support of the pelvic organs or bowel function can follow. These problems are common and usually treatable.

Conditions treated

  • Urinary incontinence — stress, urgency and mixed leakage
  • Pelvic organ prolapse — uterine and vaginal prolapse, cystocele, rectocele and vault prolapse
  • Overactive bladder and difficulty emptying the bladder
  • Recurrent problems after previous pelvic floor surgery

The two pages linked above explain symptoms, assessment and treatment in detail.

How assessment works

A consultation covers your symptoms and how they affect daily life, followed by an examination. Depending on the findings, a bladder diary, urine test, pelvic floor ultrasound or urodynamic study may be arranged.

Treatment approach

Care generally begins with conservative measures — pelvic floor physiotherapy, bladder training, pessaries or medication. Surgery, including pelvic reconstructive surgery, is discussed when these have not given enough relief or are unlikely to help.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • Urine leakage or urgency affecting daily life
  • A vaginal bulge or pelvic pressure
  • Symptoms that persist after initial treatment or previous surgery

Frequently asked questions

What does a urogynaecologist treat?

Bladder control problems, pelvic organ prolapse and related pelvic floor conditions in women.

Are these problems just part of ageing?

They become more common with age, but they are not inevitable and effective treatments are available.
