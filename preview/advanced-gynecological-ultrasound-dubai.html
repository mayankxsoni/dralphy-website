Advanced Gynaecological Ultrasound & 3D Imaging

A detailed pelvic ultrasound, interpreted by the clinician who examines you, often gives the clearest picture of what is causing your symptoms — and whether treatment is needed.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

What it is

Advanced gynaecological ultrasound is a detailed, structured scan of the uterus, ovaries and surrounding pelvic structures, usually performed transvaginally for the best image quality. 3D imaging reconstructs the shape of the uterus and its cavity.

What it can assess

  • Endometriosis mapping — endometriomas, deep nodules and whether the bowel, bladder or ligaments are involved
  • Fibroids — number, size, position and relationship to the uterine cavity
  • Adenomyosis
  • Ovarian cysts and structured assessment of their features
  • The uterine cavity — polyps, submucosal fibroids and congenital variations, sometimes with saline sonography
  • Pelvic floor ultrasound for prolapse and incontinence, where available

When it may not be appropriate or sufficient

  • A transvaginal scan is not performed without your consent or if you have not been sexually active — transabdominal or other imaging is used instead
  • Superficial endometriosis is generally not visible on any imaging
  • MRI may be added for very large fibroids or when more detail is needed for surgical planning

Preparation

  • An empty bladder is usually best for a transvaginal scan
  • Simple bowel preparation may be advised before endometriosis mapping
  • Bring previous scan and MRI reports for comparison

What to expect

The scan takes about 20–40 minutes. A slim probe with a protective cover is placed in the vagina; it may be uncomfortable but should not be painful, and you can ask to stop at any time. A chaperone is available.

How imaging informs your care

Findings are explained to you at the time, often using the images. They help decide whether observation, medication or surgery is appropriate and, when surgery is planned, allow the operation, the team and the consent discussion to be tailored in advance.

Limits and follow-up

Ultrasound is safe and involves no radiation. It is operator-dependent, and a normal scan does not exclude every condition. Repeat scans may be recommended to monitor change over time.

Frequently asked questions

Is a transvaginal ultrasound painful?

Most people find it mildly uncomfortable rather than painful. Tell the clinician if you feel pain so the examination can be adapted.

Can ultrasound diagnose endometriosis?

It can identify endometriomas and deep endometriosis in experienced hands. Superficial disease usually cannot be seen.

Do I need both ultrasound and MRI?

Often ultrasound is enough. MRI is requested when it is likely to add useful information.
