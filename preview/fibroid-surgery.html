Fibroid Surgery / Myomectomy

Myomectomy removes fibroids while keeping the uterus. The route — hysteroscopic, laparoscopic or open — depends on the size, number and position of the fibroids.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

What it is

Myomectomy is the surgical removal of fibroids with repair of the uterus.

  • Hysteroscopic myomectomy — through the cervix, for fibroids inside the uterine cavity; no abdominal incisions
  • Laparoscopic myomectomy — keyhole surgery for fibroids in the wall or on the surface of the uterus
  • Open myomectomy — through a larger incision, when fibroids are very large or numerous

Who may benefit

  • Patients with heavy bleeding or pressure symptoms from fibroids
  • Those who wish to keep the uterus or plan a future pregnancy
  • Selected patients with fibroids and infertility or pregnancy loss

When it may not be appropriate

  • Fibroids that cause no symptoms
  • When medication or a less invasive option is likely to control symptoms
  • When childbearing is complete and hysterectomy offers a more reliable solution
  • When imaging raises concern that needs a different approach

Alternatives — observation, medication, ablation techniques, uterine artery embolisation and hysterectomy — are discussed alongside surgery.

Preparation

Ultrasound, and MRI where needed, maps each fibroid to plan the route of surgery. Anaemia is corrected beforehand where possible, and medication to reduce bleeding or fibroid size may be advised for a short period.

What to expect

Surgery is under general anaesthetic. Hysteroscopic procedures are usually day cases; laparoscopic myomectomy commonly needs one night in hospital; open surgery two to three nights. Complex or multiple fibroids take longer and may need a staged approach.

Risks

Risks include bleeding (occasionally requiring transfusion), infection, adhesions, injury to nearby organs and, rarely, the need for hysterectomy to control bleeding. New fibroids can develop later.

Recovery and future pregnancy

  • Return to normal activity: a few days after hysteroscopic surgery, two to four weeks after laparoscopic surgery, around six weeks after open surgery
  • You will be advised how long to wait before trying to conceive
  • Depending on how the uterus was repaired, caesarean delivery may be recommended in a future pregnancy

Frequently asked questions

Can large or multiple fibroids be removed by keyhole surgery?

Sometimes. It depends on their size, number and position; this is assessed on imaging before a route is recommended.

Will fibroids grow back?

Removed fibroids do not regrow, but new ones can form, particularly in younger patients.

Is myomectomy safer than hysterectomy?

They are different operations with different aims and risks. The choice depends on your symptoms, plans for pregnancy and preferences.
