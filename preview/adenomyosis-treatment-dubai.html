Condition

Adenomyosis

Adenomyosis can cause heavy, painful periods and an enlarged, tender uterus. It often overlaps with fibroids and endometriosis.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

In adenomyosis, tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows within its muscular wall. The uterus may become enlarged and tender. It can be diffuse (spread through the wall) or focal (an adenomyoma).

Common symptoms

  • Heavy or prolonged periods
  • Severe period cramps
  • Pelvic heaviness or bloating
  • Pain during intercourse
  • Difficulty conceiving in some patients

Symptoms overlap with fibroids and endometriosis, and the conditions frequently occur together.

Possible causes

The cause is not fully understood. Adenomyosis is more often diagnosed in people who have had children or uterine surgery, but it is also seen in younger patients as imaging improves.

Diagnostic approach

A transvaginal ultrasound performed by an experienced operator can identify typical features of adenomyosis. MRI is added where appropriate — for example to distinguish adenomyosis from fibroids or to plan treatment.

Treatment choices

  • Pain relief and non-hormonal medicines to reduce bleeding
  • Hormonal treatment, including a hormone-releasing intrauterine system
  • Selected procedures for focal disease in carefully chosen patients
  • Hysterectomy when clinically indicated, symptoms are severe and childbearing is complete

For those wishing to conceive, treatment is planned together with a fertility specialist where relevant.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • Heavy or painful periods not controlled by first-line treatment
  • Uncertainty whether symptoms are due to fibroids, adenomyosis or endometriosis
  • Adenomyosis with fertility concerns

Frequently asked questions

Is adenomyosis the same as endometriosis?

No. They are related but different: adenomyosis is within the wall of the uterus, endometriosis is outside it. They can occur together.

Is hysterectomy the only cure?

Hysterectomy removes the affected uterus, but many patients control symptoms with medical treatment and never need it.

Can adenomyosis affect pregnancy?

It may in some patients. Individual advice depends on the extent of disease and your history.
