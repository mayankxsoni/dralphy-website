Condition

Ovarian Cysts

Most ovarian cysts are harmless and settle on their own. Ultrasound assessment helps decide which cysts can be watched and which may need treatment.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled or solid swelling in or on an ovary. Cysts are common at all ages. Most are benign and many disappear without treatment.

Common symptoms

  • Often none — many cysts are found on a scan done for another reason
  • Pelvic ache or pressure, often on one side
  • Bloating
  • Pain during intercourse

Sudden, severe pelvic pain — especially with nausea, vomiting or fainting — can indicate a twisted or ruptured cyst. Go to the nearest emergency department.

Types of cyst

  • Functional cysts — related to ovulation; usually resolve within a few cycles
  • Endometriomas — cysts caused by endometriosis
  • Dermoid cysts (mature teratomas) — benign cysts containing different tissue types
  • Cystadenomas and other benign growths
  • Cysts with features that need further assessment to exclude malignancy

Diagnostic approach

A detailed ultrasound describes the size and internal features of a cyst and allows a structured assessment of risk. Blood tests (tumour markers) and MRI may be added in selected cases. A repeat scan after a few weeks is often the most useful next step.

Treatment choices

  • Surveillance with repeat ultrasound for simple or functional cysts
  • Laparoscopic cystectomy — removing the cyst while preserving healthy ovarian tissue
  • Removal of the ovary in selected situations
  • Referral to a gynaecological oncology team when imaging suggests this is appropriate

When surgery is planned, protecting ovarian reserve and future fertility is a priority, particularly with endometriomas. The possible effect on fertility is discussed before any operation.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • A cyst that persists or grows on repeat scans
  • A cyst causing pain
  • An endometrioma or dermoid cyst
  • Any cyst with complex features on ultrasound
  • A cyst found after the menopause

Frequently asked questions

Do all ovarian cysts need surgery?

No. Most simple cysts are monitored and resolve without treatment.

Will surgery affect my fertility?

Cystectomy aims to preserve the ovary, but some reduction in ovarian reserve can occur. This is weighed against the reasons for surgery in each case.

Are ovarian cysts cancerous?

The great majority are benign. Ultrasound features, age and blood tests help identify the small number that need urgent specialist review.
