Condition

Endometriosis

Endometriosis can cause painful periods, pelvic pain and difficulty conceiving. Careful assessment helps decide which treatment — if any — is right for you.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside it — most often on the pelvic lining, ovaries, and sometimes the bowel, bladder or the ligaments behind the uterus. It affects people in different ways: some have severe symptoms, others have few.

Diagnosis is often delayed by several years because symptoms can be mistaken for "normal" period pain or for bowel and bladder problems.

Common symptoms

  • Painful periods that interfere with work, study or daily life
  • Pelvic pain between periods
  • Pain during or after intercourse
  • Pain when opening the bowels or passing urine, especially around a period
  • Heavy or irregular bleeding
  • Fatigue
  • Difficulty becoming pregnant

The severity of pain does not always match the extent of disease.

Possible causes and types

The exact cause is not fully understood; genetic, hormonal and immune factors are all thought to play a part. Endometriosis is commonly described as:

  • Superficial peritoneal disease — small deposits on the pelvic lining
  • Ovarian endometriomas — cysts containing old blood
  • Deep endometriosis — nodules that can involve the bowel, bladder, ureters or pelvic nerves

Diagnostic approach

Assessment starts with a detailed history and examination. A specialist transvaginal ultrasound can map endometriomas and deep disease and assess how freely the pelvic organs move. MRI may be added where appropriate, particularly when bowel, bladder or ureteric involvement is suspected.

A normal scan does not exclude superficial endometriosis. Laparoscopy is not always needed to start treatment.

Treatment choices

Treatment depends on your symptoms, the extent of disease, and whether you wish to become pregnant.

  • Pain relief and hormonal treatment, which can control symptoms for many patients
  • Laparoscopic excision, which may be considered when symptoms persist despite medical treatment, when there is deep disease affecting organs, or in selected fertility situations
  • Fertility care, planned together with a fertility specialist where relevant
  • Pelvic floor physiotherapy and pain management support

Complex disease involving the bowel, bladder or ureters is planned with a multidisciplinary team. Endometriosis can recur after any treatment, and surgery carries risks that will be discussed with you. Read more about endometriosis excision surgery.

Recovery

Recovery after surgery depends on the extent of the operation. Many patients go home the same day or after one night following limited surgery, and need longer after complex surgery. A follow-up visit reviews recovery, laboratory results and any further treatment.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • Period or pelvic pain that is not controlled by simple measures
  • Bowel or bladder symptoms that follow your cycle
  • An endometrioma or suspected deep endometriosis on a scan
  • Symptoms returning after previous surgery
  • Endometriosis with difficulty conceiving

Frequently asked questions

My ultrasound was normal. Can I still have endometriosis?

Yes. Superficial endometriosis is usually not visible on ultrasound or MRI. Your symptoms and examination remain important in deciding next steps.

Is surgery always necessary?

No. Many patients are managed with medical treatment. Surgery is considered when it is likely to help and after the alternatives and risks have been discussed.

Can endometriosis come back after surgery?

It can. Recurrence depends on several factors, including the type of disease and the completeness of excision. Follow-up and, in some cases, medical treatment after surgery may reduce the risk.

Does endometriosis always cause infertility?

No. Many people with endometriosis conceive naturally. If pregnancy is not happening, assessment of both partners helps to plan the most suitable approach.
