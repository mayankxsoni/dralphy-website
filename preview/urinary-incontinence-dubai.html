Condition

Urinary Incontinence

Leaking urine is common and treatable. The right treatment depends on the type of leakage and how much it affects your life.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Book a Consultation

Overview

Urinary incontinence is the unintentional loss of urine. It is not an inevitable part of ageing or childbirth, and most patients improve with treatment.

Common symptoms

  • Leaking with coughing, sneezing, laughing, lifting or exercise
  • A sudden, strong urge to pass urine, sometimes with leakage before reaching the toilet
  • Passing urine frequently, including at night
  • A combination of the above

Types

  • Stress urinary incontinence — leakage with physical effort, due to weakened support of the urethra
  • Urgency incontinence (overactive bladder) — leakage with a sudden urge
  • Mixed incontinence — features of both

Diagnostic approach

Assessment includes a history, examination of the pelvic floor, a urine test and a bladder diary recorded over a few days. Pelvic floor ultrasound may add information. Urodynamic testing is used selectively — for example before surgery or when the diagnosis is unclear.

Treatment choices

  • Supervised pelvic floor muscle training with a specialist physiotherapist
  • Bladder training and adjustments to fluids, caffeine and weight
  • Medication, when relevant, for urgency symptoms
  • Urethral bulking injections for stress incontinence
  • Sling procedures and other operations for stress incontinence

Conservative treatment is normally tried first. Every procedure has realistic benefits and risks — including the possibility that symptoms persist, return, or that new bladder symptoms develop — and these are discussed before you decide.

When specialist care is appropriate

  • Leakage that affects exercise, work or confidence
  • Symptoms that persist after pelvic floor training
  • Leakage together with a vaginal bulge or recurrent urine infections
  • Blood in the urine or pain — which need prompt assessment

Frequently asked questions

Is leaking urine normal after childbirth?

It is common, but it is not something you simply have to accept. Assessment and treatment are available.

Will I need surgery?

Many patients improve with physiotherapy and bladder training alone. Procedures are discussed when these have not helped enough.

What is a bladder diary?

A simple record, kept for a few days, of what you drink, when you pass urine and when leakage happens. It helps identify the type of incontinence.
